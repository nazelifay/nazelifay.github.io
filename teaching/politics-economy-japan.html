Politics and Economy of Japan

Course Description

When the United States defeated Japan in World War II, few predicted that this small island nation would become an economic powerhouse. By the end of the 1970s, Japan was running a massive trade surplus with the U.S., prompting Western scholars to ask whether it had developed a distinctive model of capitalism. The 1980s boom made Japan the world's second-largest economy. Japan's fortunes reversed in the 1990s with the onset of the "Lost Decades," a prolonged recession accompanied by corporate scandals, a rapidly aging society, falling fertility rates, political corruption, and rising nationalism.

This course surveys the key features of Japanese politics, society, and economy — but it is more than a course about Japan. Students learn to view U.S. foreign policy from a Japanese perspective, and use Japan as an early case study of demographic and social trends now unfolding across East Asia and beyond, from declining marriage and fertility rates to rapid population aging. Course materials include films, documentaries, manga, anime, and newspaper articles alongside academic texts.
