Bio

I am a PhD candidate in political science at Syracuse University in the Maxwell School of Citizenship and Public Affairs, working at the intersection of Distributive Politics and Political Behavior.

My dissertation investigates how constituency level voting behavior shapes strategic distribution and continued maintenance of public goods, and what these dynamics mean for political accountability and government responsiveness, with a regional focus on Europe. To this end, I employ a multi-method approach that combines an original, web-scraped dataset tracking the cancellation and delay trajectories of different infrastructure projects with survey data capturing public opinion reactions to these outcomes.

In other ongoing work, I examine how citizens evaluate their representation amidst the multiplicity of representative bodies. This work aims to understand the trade-offs of partisan (mis)alignment across different representative bodies and how these trade-offs shape satisfaction with democracy, drawing on survey experiments conducted in the UK and Turkiye.

I hold an M.A. in Political Science from Syracuse University. I earned my bachelor's degree at Koç University, where I double-majored in Political Science and Sociology and graduated as class valedictorian in 2022.
